## Overview

A fisheye lens is one of the most fun ways to change how your photos look. It bends straight lines into sweeping curves and squeezes an almost impossible amount of the scene into one frame. The problem is that most fisheye lenses from big camera brands cost several hundred dollars.

The **7Artisans 7.5mm f/2.8 Mark II** fixes that. It is currently **7Artisans' best-selling product**, and it is easy to see why: a 190° field of view, an f/2.8 aperture, upgraded ED optics and a metal body for **around $118**.

This review is based on the official specifications, the lens design and its features compared with other options in the category.

## Design and build quality

The Mark II is built almost entirely from metal and feels far more premium than its price suggests. At **about 265 g**, it is still small and light enough to leave on your camera all day or pack for travel.

![7Artisans 7.5mm f/2.8 Mark II close-up details on a mirrorless camera](/assets/img/products/7artisans-7-5mm-f2-8-ii-details.webp)

The **aperture ring is de-clicked**, so it turns smoothly and silently. That is a real advantage for video, because you can change exposure mid-shot without a click or a sudden jump. A distance scale on the focus ring helps you set focus quickly without looking through the viewfinder.

The front has a **built-in petal hood** that protects the bulging front element. The trade-off is that you cannot attach standard screw-in filters.

## Image quality

The Mark II brings two meaningful optical upgrades over the original:

- **New Hoya glass** for better clarity and contrast.
- **Two ED (extra-low dispersion) elements** that reduce color fringing toward the edges, the area where cheap fisheye lenses usually struggle most.

The optical design uses **11 elements in 9 groups**. As with most budget ultra-wide lenses, you can expect the center to be sharpest and the extreme edges to be softer wide open, and stopping down to around f/5.6–f/8 is the usual way to get the most even results.

The **f/2.8 maximum aperture** is a big plus for night photography. Combined with the super-wide view, it lets you capture the Milky Way, star trails or auroras with shorter exposures and less noise.

## Using a manual fisheye

Manual focus sounds intimidating, but a 7.5 mm fisheye is one of the easiest lenses to focus manually. The depth of field is enormous: **set focus just short of infinity, choose f/5.6 or f/8, and almost everything from near to far will be sharp.**

> **Tip:** Turn on focus peaking and magnification for close-up creative shots. The lens focuses down to just 15 cm, which lets you get dramatically close to your subject.

Because the lens has no electronic contacts, your camera will not record aperture data, and you need to enable your camera's **"Release shutter without lens"** setting the first time you use it.

## What it is best for

- **Creative and street photography:** exaggerated perspectives and curved horizons that grab attention.
- **Action and skate videos:** the classic fisheye look, up close.
- **Astrophotography and landscapes:** a huge slice of sky in a single frame at f/2.8.
- **Architecture and interiors:** capture an entire room or building when you cannot step back.
- **Pets and portraits:** playful, funny close-ups with big noses and wide backgrounds.

## Compatibility

The lens is designed for **APS-C** sensors and is available for **Sony E** (a6000 series, ZV-E10, FX30), **Fujifilm X**, **Nikon Z** (Z50, Zfc, Z30), **Canon RF** (R7, R10, R50, R100), **Canon EF-M** and **Micro Four Thirds** (Panasonic and Olympus/OM System). Choose the correct mount when ordering. On Micro Four Thirds the smaller sensor gives a slightly narrower field of view.
