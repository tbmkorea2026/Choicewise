## Quick verdict

- **Choose Satin** if your skin is **normal to combination**: some shine in the T-zone, or you dislike rich creams.
- **Choose Velvet** if your skin is **normal to dry**: tightness, flakiness, or you want a more cocooning feel.

If you are unsure, think about how your skin feels **a few hours after cleansing** without any products: shiny points to Satin, tight points to Velvet.

## What they share

According to Sothys, both creams combine **two patented Sothys actives**, organic **rowan berry peptides** and **boletus (mushroom) extract**, with **high-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid**. Sothys describes the goal as a "dual protective and treatment action": plumping the skin with hydration and helping it keep that moisture. Both also contain humectants such as **glycerin, urea, hydroxyethyl urea and sodium PCA**.

## Where they differ

The difference is in the **cream base**, which determines texture and richness:

- **Satin** relies more on lightweight **esters and oils** (caprylic/capric triglyceride, C12-15 alkyl benzoate, isononyl isononanoate) near the top of its ingredient list.
- **Velvet** places richer emollients, including **shea butter**, **squalane**, **hydrogenated polydecene** and **octyldodecanol**, near the top of its list.

Both formulas technically contain shea butter and squalane, but in Satin they appear close to the end of the ingredient list. Ingredients are listed roughly in descending order of concentration, so they play a much smaller role there.

That is why Sothys assigns Satin to normal-to-combination skin and Velvet to normal-to-dry skin.
