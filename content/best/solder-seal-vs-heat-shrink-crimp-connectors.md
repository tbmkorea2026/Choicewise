## Quick verdict

- **Choose solder seal connectors** for **quick inline splices** when you don't own a crimper, or when you like seeing the joint through the clear sleeve.
- **Choose heat shrink crimp terminals** when you need **ring, spade, bullet or fork** terminals, connections you can unplug, or a **mechanical crimp**, for example in high-vibration or marine applications.

Many DIYers keep both in their toolbox.

## How solder seal connectors work

Each connector is a **clear heat shrink sleeve** with a **solder ring** in the middle and **adhesive rings** at each end. Strip both wires, slide them in from each end so they overlap under the solder ring, then heat evenly. The solder melts and joins the wires, the sleeve shrinks, and the adhesive seals the ends. Haisstronica says the dual-wall tubing protects against moisture, dirt and corrosion.

**Why people like them:** no crimper is needed, and you can see whether the solder has flowed through the clear sleeve.

**Watch out for:** uneven or insufficient heat. Heat until the solder visibly flows into the strands, and avoid scorching the tubing.

## How heat shrink crimp terminals work

These are **copper terminals** covered with **adhesive-lined 3:1 heat shrink**. You crimp the terminal onto the stripped wire with a compatible crimper, such as Haisstronica's [AWG 22-10 Ratchet Crimping Tool](/reviews/haisstronica-ratchet-crimping-tool-review/), and then heat it so the tubing shrinks and the adhesive seals the joint.

**Why people like them:** a mechanical crimp, plus many terminal shapes for batteries, studs, screw blocks and plug-in connections.

**Watch out for:** using the wrong crimper or die position. Match the terminal color to the marked crimp position and tug-test every connection.
