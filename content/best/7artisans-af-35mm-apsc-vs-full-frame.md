## Quick verdict

- **You shoot an APS-C Sony** (a6000-series, ZV-E10, FX30) and mostly take photos → buy the **APS-C version**. It gives you the same field of view for **less than half the price** and weighs only about 180 g.
- **You shoot a full-frame Sony** (a7-series, FX3), make a lot of **video**, or plan to **upgrade to full-frame** → buy the **full-frame version**.

## The key differences

### 1. Which camera you own

This is the deciding factor. An APS-C lens projects a smaller image circle. On a full-frame Sony you would have to use crop mode, giving up resolution. The full-frame lens works on **both** camera types.

On an APS-C camera, though, **both lenses give the same ≈52.5 mm equivalent view**, a classic "normal" perspective for street, travel and everyday shots. In that situation, the cheaper APS-C lens makes a lot of sense.

### 2. Video features

The full-frame version is clearly aimed at hybrid creators. Its **stepless aperture ring** lets you change exposure smoothly and silently while recording, and the **FN button** and **photo/video switch** add handy controls. 7Artisans also says it minimizes focus breathing.

### 3. Size, weight and price

The APS-C lens is the lightweight, budget option at about **180 g and $106**. The full-frame lens costs **$133 more**, and 7Artisans describes it as having a **high-strength metal body** with a more complex optical design.

## Can't decide?

If you use an APS-C camera today but expect to move to full-frame within a year or two, the full-frame version can save you from buying twice. Otherwise, take the APS-C lens and spend the savings on a second focal length; 7Artisans also sells the same APS-C design in **25mm and 50mm**, or all three as a set for $315.
