## Flow and water waste

Both produce far more than a household drinks, so the GPD rating mostly shows up as **how fast a glass fills**. The bigger practical difference is **water waste**: with **3:1**, the G3P800 sends about one cup to the drain for every three filtered; the G3P600's **2:1** sends one for every two.

## Filtration and certification

Both use a **0.0001 μm RO membrane** and are **certified by IAPMO R&T to NSF/ANSI 58** (RO performance) and **372** (low lead), plus **42** (taste and chlorine), per Waterdrop. The G3P800 adds a **UV sterilizer** and lists **NSF/ANSI 53** on its spec table, though other parts of the site list only 42, 58 and 372. Check each model's performance data sheet for the contaminants you care about.

## Running costs

Same filter schedule: **CF** every 6 months, **CB** every 12 months (both $29.99), **RO** every 24 months. The G3P800's RO filter costs **$30 more**, so it runs about **$15 a year more** on Waterdrop's estimate, offset slightly by less wastewater.

## Before you buy either

- **Test your water** first; well water may need extra treatment.
- **Power and plumbing:** both need an outlet under the sink and connections to the cold-water line and drain.
- **Returns:** 30 days with no restocking fee, but you pay return shipping unless it's defective.
- **Warranty:** 1 year on both (the [X12](/reviews/waterdrop-x12-review/) gets 2 years).

## The bottom line

The **G3P600** is the sensible pick for most homes: the same certified RO filtration, the same size and faucet, for much less money. Step up to the **G3P800** if you want **less wastewater, a UV stage and faster flow** for a bigger household.
