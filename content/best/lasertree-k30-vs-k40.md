## Quick verdict

- **Want strong cuts for less, or have a lighter machine?** Get the **K30** ($389).
- **Want the most power your machine can carry, with a listed fine spot?** Get the **K40** ($539).

Code **PARKCHOICE** takes 5% off either at the time of writing. Both are Class 4 lasers: wear 450 nm goggles and use an enclosure.

## Power and cutting

The K40's two extra diodes give it about a third more optical power. LaserTree publishes one-pass cutting data for the K30, including **15 mm plywood at 180 mm/min**, but we didn't find comparable figures on the K40 page. More power generally means fewer passes or faster cuts in thick wood, but your results depend on material, focus and air assist.

## Detail and focus

The K40 is the one with a **listed spot size (0.12 × 0.16 mm)**, which matters for fine engraving. It also has a **115 mm dovetail rail** for a wide focus range and a **detachable cover**. The K30 has a simpler focus bar and a temperature display with a 55 °C buzzer alarm.

## Fit and weight

Both include an adapter board and mounting plate. The K40 needs **6A instead of 5A** and is **190 g heavier**. On a lightweight hobby frame, that extra weight can affect speed and accuracy, so check your machine's limits.

## Ratings and warranty

The K30 has **13 reviews** averaging 4.8; the K40 has **one**. Both pages promise a **one-year warranty**, while LaserTree's replacement policy covers **three months** with fees after 15 days. See our [LaserTree review](/reviews/lasertree-review/) for details.

## The bottom line

For most hobby engravers, the **K30** is the more sensible upgrade: cheaper, lighter and better reviewed. Choose the **K40** only if your machine can handle 6A and 900 g and you want the extra power and listed fine spot.
