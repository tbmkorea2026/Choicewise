## Size and space

This is the deciding factor. The **Indoor Monkey Bars** is about **35 cm taller and 38 cm wider**, and sticks out 12 cm further from the wall. That gives a longer run of monkey bars, but it needs a ceiling well above 229 cm so a child on top of the frame has headroom. The **Climbing Monkey Bars** fits rooms with standard ceilings more easily. Measure the wall and the floor area in front of it, and leave space for mats.

## Features

Both frames have a **wooden ladder** on one side, a **rope climbing net** on the other and **monkey bars** across the top. The **Indoor Monkey Bars** adds a **pull-up bar**, which older kids may use more as they grow. Both can be extended with Wood and Hearts' rope accessories, slides, mats, pod swing and foam blocks, sold separately.

## Materials and safety

Both are made of **solid beech and birch plywood** with water-based paint and lacquer, according to Wood and Hearts, and both have a **60 kg** use limit. The store's FAQ says the frame **must be anchored** to brick, concrete, solid studs or metal profiles. Both product pages also contain a generic line saying the product "easily folds"; we'd treat these as fixed frames and ask support if folding matters to you.

## Price and warranty

The Indoor Monkey Bars costs **$118 more** in either finish. Both carry Wood and Hearts' **2-year warranty** on wooden parts and **10 years** on wooden rungs. Change-of-mind returns within 30 days mean paying shipping both ways from Ukraine, so measure before you buy.

## The bottom line

If your room is tall enough, the **Indoor Monkey Bars** gives more climbing and a pull-up bar for a modest extra cost, and it's the one in the store's "Most Loved" list. If space or budget is tight, the **Climbing Monkey Bars** has the same core features in a smaller frame.
