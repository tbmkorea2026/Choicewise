## The 8-point checklist

### 1. Where does the power come from?

"Wireless" usually means the **video** is wireless. Check the **power**:

- **Battery + solar camera** (Solar 3A, Solar 5): nothing to wire at the back.
- **Reverse-light powered camera** (W7): about 30 minutes of DIY wiring at the tail light.
- **The monitor** always needs USB or a 12V socket. AUTO-VOX's Solar3A Plus spec table lists the monitor as "DC 5V (USB powered)".

### 2. Is the range right for your vehicle?

| Vehicle | Range you need | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Car, SUV, pickup | Short | Solar3A Plus (52 ft), W7 (44 ft) |
| Truck + trailer, RV | Long | Solar 5A (300 ft), Solar 5B (524 ft), WF4 (500 ft) |

Claimed range is usually line-of-sight. Leave headroom.

### 3. What does the battery claim actually measure?

"3–4 months per charge" (Solar 3A) and "up to 20 hours of use" (Solar 5) sound wildly different, but one counts days of normal reversing and the other continuous running time. Ask yourself: **will the camera be on only when reversing, or all the time while driving?**

### 4. Plate mount or magnet?

Plate brackets stay put and suit daily drivers. Magnets suit RVs, rentals and moving a camera between vehicles. Fibreglass RV backs need a metal plate (the Solar 5B includes one).

### 5. Do you want recording?

Some monitors record (AUTO-VOX Solar3A Plus Max, Solar 5B, WF4); others don't (Solar3A Plus). If you want evidence of a parking bump, check for "DVR" or "loop recording".

### 6. Viewing angle: which number?

Marketing often quotes the **diagonal** angle; spec tables may give the **horizontal** one. The Solar3A Plus lists 140° in its bullets and 110° horizontal in its spec table. Compare like with like.
