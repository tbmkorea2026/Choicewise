## Mount: plate bracket vs magnet

A **plate bracket** is semi-permanent: it stays on the car and is harder to steal or knock off. A **magnet** goes on in seconds and comes off just as fast, so you can move it between a truck and a trailer, or take it inside at a campsite. The Solar 5B includes a **metal plate** for fibreglass or aluminium RV backs.

## Range: why it matters on trailers

AUTO-VOX quotes **52 ft** for Solar 3A and **300–524 ft** for Solar 5. On a car, the camera is maybe 15 ft from the monitor. On a 35 ft fifth wheel plus the tow vehicle, a 52 ft signal is too close to its limit. Claimed ranges are usually line-of-sight, so leave headroom.

## Battery: very different claims

"3–4 months per charge" and "20 hours of use" aren't comparable. The first assumes you reverse a few times a day; the second is continuous running time. Both cameras top up from a small solar panel, but neither is meant to run all day without charging.

## Recording

Want footage after a scrape? Only the **Plus Max** and **5B** record. The Solar3A Plus's spec table says storage isn't supported.
