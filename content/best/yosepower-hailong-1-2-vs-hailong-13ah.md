## Quick verdict

- **Your old battery has a 5-pin gold-plated round plug, or you ride a Mifa, Prophete or similar bike listed on the page?** Get the **13Ah Hailong** (€209).
- **Building a DIY e-bike, or replacing a 4-pin Hailong 1-2?** Get the **Hailong 1-2**, ideally the **20Ah, 740Wh** version (€259).

## Capacity and range

The Hailong 1-2 stores **577Wh** or **740Wh**, compared with **481Wh** for the 13Ah. That's about **20% more** for the 15.6Ah version and **54% more** for the 20Ah version. Real range depends on motor, assist level, terrain and rider weight, and Yose Power doesn't publish range figures for either.

## Plug and fit

This is the deciding point. Yose Power states that the Hailong 1-2's **4-pin** port is totally different from the 13Ah battery's **5-pin gold-plated round plug**. The 13Ah case is also listed at **92 mm** tall in its dimension drawing, against **111 mm** for the Hailong 1-2, so the bigger pack may not fit a tight frame. Measure your frame and check your old battery's plug before ordering.

## Power and charging

The Hailong 1-2 lists a **30A** maximum current and kits up to **900W**, versus **20A** and **700W** for the 13Ah. For a legal 250W pedelec both have plenty. The Hailong 1-2 also includes a faster **3A** charger instead of 2A.

## Ratings and warranty

The 13Ah is rated **4.81 from 48 reviews**, the Hailong 1-2 **4.67 from 30**, with lower-rated reviews mentioning a hot charger and a power issue. Both carry an **18-month** battery warranty, but after 30 days you pay shipping on claims. See our [Yose Power review](/reviews/yosepower-review/) for the store's return rules.

## The bottom line

Let your plug decide. If you need the **5-pin round plug**, the **13Ah Hailong** is the cheaper, better-rated fit. If you're building a new bike or have a 4-pin mount, the **Hailong 1-2** gives far more capacity and a faster charger for €10–€50 more.
