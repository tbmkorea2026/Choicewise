## Quick verdict

- **Choose the PST** if your speakers are rated **85–94 dB** (most bookshelf and tower speakers), you want the best value, or your speakers are compact.
- **Choose the RST** if your speakers are **94–96 dB**, you specifically want a **true aluminum ribbon**, or you have larger towers that suit a bigger unit.

For most home systems, the **PST is the smarter buy**. Read our full [PST review](/reviews/aperion-pst-super-tweeter-review/) for setup tips.

## The key differences

### 1. Driver type

The **PST** uses a **planar-magnetic** driver: a thin Kapton film with a printed aluminum conductor pattern. The **RST** uses a **true ribbon**, a single strip of aluminum only 20 μm thick suspended in a magnetic field. Both are low-mass designs intended for fast, detailed treble.

### 2. Speaker matching

This is the most practical difference. A super tweeter must play at roughly the same level as your speakers. The PST is designed for speakers up to **94 dB**, while the RST's higher **96 dB** sensitivity stretches its match to speakers up to **96 dB**. Check your speaker's spec sheet before you choose.

### 3. Size and weight

The RST is noticeably bigger and **more than twice as heavy** (3.9 lb vs 1.7 lb per unit). That makes the PST easier to place on small bookshelf speakers, while the RST suits larger towers.

### 4. Price

At **$399 vs $649 a pair**, the PST saves you **$250**, enough to put toward speaker stands or cables.

## What they have in common

Both models share Aperion's super tweeter feature set: the same **8–40 kHz** range, **five crossover points plus OFF**, a **0 to −5 dB treble trim**, connection through your speakers' existing binding posts with **no extra amplifier**, a **3-year warranty** with lifetime support, and Aperion's **30-day in-home audition**.
